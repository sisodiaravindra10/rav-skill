Weekend wheel classes

Three hours, one wheel, two pots to keep.

No experience needed, and nobody is watching. You get your own wheel, a kilo and a half of clay, and a teacher for every three people. Most first-timers centre the clay by the second try and pull a proper bowl before chai.

  • 1
    Wedge and centreLearn to knead the air out and sit the clay dead centre. This is the hard part, and we stay with you through it.
  • 2
    Open and pullThumb in, walls up. You will throw four or five pieces and choose the two you like best.
  • 3
    Chai on the terraceKulhad chai and kachori from the bazaar while your pots stiffen in the sun.
  • 4
    Pick a glaze, leave the rest to usWe trim, glaze and fire your two pieces. Collect them in three weeks or we ship them anywhere in India.

How a plate gets made

Nineteen days from mud to table.

  1. 01

    Throw

    A ball of stoneware clay, a wheel, wet hands. A dinner plate takes about six minutes once you have made a few thousand.

  2. 02

    Trim and dry

    Next morning we turn the foot and stamp the base. Then it sits on the shelf for a week. Jaipur air does the work.

  3. 900°C

    First fire

    The bisque firing turns clay into ceramic. It comes out pale, chalky and thirsty for glaze.

  4. 1,220°C

    Glaze fire

    Dipped by hand, fired for fourteen hours, cooled for a day and a half. We open the kiln on Thursdays. Come and watch.
