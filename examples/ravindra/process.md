# How Ravindra works

An example profile: the real process of the designer who made this skill, from an interview on 19 Nov 2026. It shows what `profile/process.md` looks like when it is done. In his own install this file sits in `profile/` and outranks every rule in the core. In yours it is only an example: do not apply it to your work, run `/rav setup` and get your own.

Lines in quotation marks are his words. Lines marked *(reading)* are an interpretation that he has not confirmed; treat them as a good guess and drop them if he says otherwise.

## The order he works in

1. **A place or a memory.** "I start from a feeling." Asked what the feeling is made of: "a place or memory." It is not written down. "I just carry it in my head."
2. **References.** "Then look for references from Pinterest or other places."
3. **Several moodboards.** "Grab those, make multiple moodboards."
4. **One is chosen by feel.** "Select the one which I feel would go perfectly." The others lose because "they looked like something I'd seen before, or too boring and old."
5. **The hero first, finished.** The first thing built is "the hero", and "I finish it properly before moving on."
6. **Step away, come back.** The hero is judged cold: "I step away and come back, gut reaction." No checklist.
7. **Then the rest of the page,** each element pushed and then pulled back (see below).

## What the skill does because of it

- **Ask for the place first.** Since the starting point lives in his head and nowhere else, intake's first question on a new surface is the place or memory behind it. One sentence from him here is worth more than every other answer. If he has none yet, offer two or three places drawn from the subject and let him react.
- **Directions before a build.** He chooses between boards, so give him boards. For a new surface, put two or three distinct directions in front of him, each as a real composition (generate mode, or a quick rendered hero), not as adjectives. Never arrive with one.
- **Freshness is the filter.** A direction is cut if it looks like something already seen, or boring, or dated. That is his test, ahead of polish or correctness. Before showing a direction, ask whether he has seen this page before. If the honest answer is yes, replace it.
- **Hero, then stop.** Build the hero to a finished state before any other section exists: real type, real image, real motion, both breakpoints. Then show it and wait. This is the one planned pause in a build, and it is where his gut reaction happens. Do not build the page underneath a hero he has not reacted to.
- **Give the cold look a chance.** He judges after stepping away. So show the hero as a clean screenshot or a link with nothing else attached, without a list of what was done and why. Explanation warms the look up and spoils the test.
- **When he is not there to react,** finish the hero, get a fresh critic's first reaction in one sentence in place of his, and continue. Say that the pause was skipped.

## Push, then pull back

"I had ideated too much on the heading style, which got me a very cluttered design style. Then later I came to this, a bit designed but minimal kind of approach."

That is how he gets to a finished element: too far first, then back. The two-weight headings on a security company site he designed are what survived that. One idea stays, the rest goes.

- Explore an element past the point of good sense before settling it. A first idea that was never pushed is usually the default.
- Then cut until one idea is left and the element reads as "a bit designed but minimal".
- The failure he named is clutter from too many ideas in one element. The failure he rejects elsewhere is no thought at all. The target sits between: one considered idea per element, clearly visible.

## What he refuses on sight

In his words:

- "Generic stock photos"
- "Too many gradients"
- "Boring layouts"
- "Not using the images dynamically"
- "Not putting enough thought in each element"

## Images used dynamically

Asked what this means, he pointed at a site he designed for a security company. *(reading, from looking at that site; he confirmed it is his work, not each point below)*

- **Cut out, never boxed.** The eagle, the dog in sunglasses and the towers are lifted off their backgrounds. No image sits in a plain rectangle.
- **Breaking the container.** A wing crosses a band and leaves the page. A head rises out of the top of its card. Towers run through two cells of a grid.
- **Carrying across sections.** The eagle holds its place while the page scrolls and the ground changes from light to dark behind it.
- **An unexpected subject.** A security company shown through an eagle and a dog in aviators. The expected picture, a padlock or a server room, is the generic stock photo he refuses.
- **Part of the composition.** The towers stand over a field of repeated type, so image and grid are one thing.

The lazy version: a stock photo inside a rounded rectangle, sitting still, showing what the heading already says.

This needs real cut-outs. A circle or polygon mask standing in for a subject's edge is worse than no effect (SKILL.md, Imagery).

## The four things he checks

Asked for his own rules, he gave four words: "contrast, visual balance, visual hierarchy, make it pop." As questions to ask of any screen:

- **Contrast.** Is there a real difference between the big thing and the small thing, the heavy and the light, the loud color and the quiet ground? Or is everything medium?
- **Visual balance.** Does the weight of the page sit right? A heavy image on one side needs something answering it on the other, even if that something is empty space.
- **Visual hierarchy.** Is it obvious in one second what to look at first, second and third?
- **Make it pop.** Is there one thing on this screen a person would remember? If nothing pops, nothing was decided. See Conviction before compliance in SKILL.md.

## His own work as worked examples

- **A security company site** (he designed it; the name is kept out of this file at his request): two-weight display headings, cut-out subjects that break their containers, a bento of unequal cells with one saturated cell among quiet ones, a light-to-dark ground change tied together by one image.
- **The live portfolio, Suryanagari:** a place and a memory made into a system. Jodhpur as the Sun City, one sun that lights everything.
- **The shutter unlock:** a physical thing pushed all the way, corrugated metal, weight, three seconds.

## What this file does not yet know

The interview was short, and several answers were chosen from options offered to him, so they may be narrower than the truth. Still open, worth asking when it comes up naturally:

- How he works with a client's feedback, and when he pushes back.
- How he decides a whole page, not just a hero, is finished.
- What he does when the gut reaction is "no" but he cannot say why.
- How type and color get chosen once the board is picked.
- Which of the five image moves matter most to him.

When he answers any of these in passing, add it here in his words.
